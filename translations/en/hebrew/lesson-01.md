## 1. The Alphabet

## 2. Final forms

Five letters are written differently at the end of a word. The shape changes; the pronunciation and transliteration do not.

- כ → ך, as in דרך “road”
- מ → ם, as in עם “people”
- נ → ן, as in זקן “elder”
- פ → ף, as in כסף “silver”
- צ → ץ

## 3. The begadkefat letters

Six consonants have two pronunciations each — a stop and a fricative. What distinguishes them is a point inside the letter, the dagesh lene: with the dagesh the sound is a stop, without it a fricative. The dagesh lene occurs only in these six letters.

- **Stops:** בּ גּ דּ כּ פּ תּ
- **Fricatives:** ב ג ד כ פ ת

## 4. Gutturals

There are four guttural consonants: א, ע, ה and ח. The consonant ר also often behaves like a guttural. Gutturals are never doubled — this will matter in chapter 5.
