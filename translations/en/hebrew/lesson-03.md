# Chapter 3 — Syllables and Reading Rules

> Working English translation of the Russian grammar in `data/hebrew-lessons.js`.
> Hebrew forms are copied verbatim out of the data, never retyped. The text has
> not yet been checked against Pratico & Van Pelt, *Basics of Biblical Hebrew Grammar*.

## 1. Two rules of syllabification

- Every syllable begins with a consonant and has exactly one vowel. A syllable cannot begin with a vowel. The word דָּבָר “word” is divided thus: דָּ|בָר.
- There are only two types of syllable: open and closed. An open syllable ends in a vowel, a closed one in a consonant. In דָּ|בָר the first syllable is open and the second closed.

## 2. Stress

Stress falls, as a rule, on the last syllable, and is then left unmarked. If it falls elsewhere, a sign is placed over the stressed syllable: סֵ֫פֶר “book” (סֵ֫|פֶר).

## 3. Dagesh and syllabification

A dagesh lene does not affect syllabification, a dagesh forte does: the doubled consonant serves both as the end of the first syllable and as the beginning of the second. In the word אַתָּה “you” this gives ʾat-tāh. How to tell which dagesh stands in a begadkefat letter:

- forte — after a vowel (אַתָּה: before תּ stands patach)
- lene — after a consonant (מַלְכָּה: before כּ there is ל with a silent shewa)
- lene — at the beginning of a word (דָּבָר)

## 4. The shewa and syllabification

It all comes down to one thing: a shewa is not read if a short vowel precedes it; in all other cases it is read. In detail — the shewa is not read:

- after a short vowel: פַּרְעֹה
- the first of two adjacent shewas: מִשְׁפְּטֵי (מִשְׁ|פְּ|טֵי)
- at the end of a word: כָּתַבְתְּ
- under a guttural: שָׁמַעְתָּ

The shewa is read:

- under the first letter of a word: בְּרָכָה
- the second of two adjacent shewas: מִשְׁפְּטֵי
- under a letter with a dagesh forte: הַמְּלָכִים
- after a long vowel: כֹּתְבִים

## 5. Qamets and qamets hatuf

The signs look identical, but qamets is long ā and qamets hatuf is short o. Qamets is far more common, and in doubt it is the one to choose. The rules:

- qamets hatuf — only in a closed unstressed syllable: חָכְמָה “wisdom”, most often the word כָּל “all”
- qamets — in an open pretonic or a closed stressed syllable: both vowels in דָּבָר
- the meteg sign (a short stroke under the letter) occurs with qamets, not with qamets hatuf: בָּֽתִּים “houses”
